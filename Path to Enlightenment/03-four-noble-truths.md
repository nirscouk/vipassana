# Chapter 3 · The disease, the cause, the cure, the medicine

The Four Noble Truths are the whole of the path, turned like a jewel. Everything else is commentary and training.

| Truth | The physician’s word | Your work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| There is suffering | The disease | Know it fully, in the body |
| It has a cause | The diagnosis | Stop feeding the cause |
| It can end | The prognosis | Taste even small cessations |
| There is a path | The medicine | Walk it every day |

*Four tasks. Not four opinions.*

Suffering, *dukkha*, is larger than tragedy. It is the leak in every compounded thing: the pleasant that will not stay, the painful that will not leave when we push, the dull stretch of life we sleep through until it turns. Even a good day has a fine crack in it — the knowledge that it is already passing. Desire itself, watched closely, is already a tightness. Is desire dukkha? Do not answer from a book. Feel the wanting as sensation.

The cause is *taṇhā*, craving, and craving is the mother of aversion. We reach; when the world does not obey, we burn. We push; when the unwanted returns, we burn again. This is not a moral scolding. It is mechanics. Each reaction carves a groove. The groove becomes a personality. The personality becomes a life.

The end of suffering is not a better arrangement of the same fever. It is the fever going out — Nibbāna, the unconditioned, beyond the field of mind and matter where sense doors operate as they do now. You may not see that shore yet. You can already see small versions: the moment you do not answer anger, and the body cools; the moment a plan dies and you are still whole.

The path is the Eightfold Path, gathered into three trainings. For seven years or for seven days, the same medicine is given: sīla, samādhi, paññā.

The work with feeling has its own deepening. First you know *vedanā* is there. Then you explore the whole field of it. Then suffering is so thoroughly known that it can be laid down. Pleasant thought and unpleasant thought, pleasant body and unpleasant body — all of it is to be seen, not rearranged.

**Today.** The next time something small goes wrong — a delay, a cold look, a message not returned — pause before the story. Find the sensation in the chest, the belly, or the jaw. Name it silently: this is dukkha; this is the cause arising. Do not fix it for thirty seconds. That pause is already the path.
