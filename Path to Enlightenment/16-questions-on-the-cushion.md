# Chapter 16 · The questions that rise on the cushion

A living path produces living questions. Keep them as questions until the body answers.

### Is desire itself suffering?

Watch the wanting as heat, lean, and story. See whether peace increases when the wanting is believed or when it is merely known. Let that be your scripture.

### Why does desire arrive in the middle of a good sitting?

Because the deep mind is still doing its old job. Attraction is not a failure of the method. It is the method showing you the material. Feel it. Do not date it.

### How do I meditate as I fall asleep?

Breath, palm, or foot. Simple object. No campaign against sleep.

### Why is home weaker than the centre?

Home is your museum of habits. Practise anyway. Equanimity with a modest sitting is better Dhamma than craving for last year’s flow.

### What if I cannot feel anything?

Nothing obvious is also a truth. Stay. The demand to feel fireworks is desire. Subtle awareness is the path.

### Will “me” dissolve? Who is looking while I scan?

Yes — the *idea* of a solid, unchanging owner dissolves. You do not vanish from existence. The illusion of a little emperor sitting inside the head, looking out at a separate world, is what cannot be found when the looking is honest.

While you scan it feels like “I am looking at my leg.” Look closer. There is matter (*rūpa*), feeling (*vedanā*), recognition (*saññā*), the habit that wants to pull or push (*saṅkhāra*), and bare knowing (*viññāṇa*). There is no sixth item called The Person. The five processes run so fast they blur, like spokes of a wheel looking like a solid disc.

If a taste of this comes and it is peaceful, do not grab it. If it is frightening, that fear is also sensation. Stay with it. Chapter 21 takes this further.

**Today.** Write one question your practice has actually asked — not a clever question, a sore one. Sit with it for ten minutes as sensation in the body. Do not solve it. Let it be seen. Insight is what remains when the argument gets tired.
