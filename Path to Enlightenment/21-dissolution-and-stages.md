# Part VI · Where the path is going

Practice is not aimed at an experience. Experiences come and go. The aim is the end of the need to react — and, finally, the end of the fire.

---

# Chapter 21 · Dissolution, no-self, and the stages of freedom

There may come a time when the solid body is felt as a field of tiny events — *bhaṅga*, dissolution. *Anattā* becomes obvious: no I, no mine. If this comes, do not grasp the bliss of it. Dissolution clung to is another line in stone. If it does not come, do not counterfeit it. Work with solidity. The Buddha did not ask you to prefer a movie of emptiness to the truth of this moment.

### What is dissolving

Two things, not your existence.

**The sense of a solid body.** Daily life paints the hand as a permanent object of skin and bone. When attention is fine, that picture thins. You may feel a swirl of tiny events — *kalāpas* — appearing and vanishing. The “slab” becomes heat, vibration, flow. From a distance a river is a blue line on a map. Step in, and there is no river-object — only water moving.

**The story of me.** We spend years defending a character: memories, future fears, labels (*I am anxious; I am successful*), likes and dislikes. Track *anicca* long enough and the foundation of that character is seen as temporary chemical weather and thoughts that arise from nowhere and vanish. Look for the owner. Find open knowing, not a statue.

A movie looks like one continuous life. At the projector it is frames with gaps. Vipassanā raises the frame-rate of attention until the gaps are obvious. When the illusion of solidity thins, the suffering that was spent protecting a self that was never solid thins with it.

Who is looking? Not a sixth person. Consciousness knowing a sensation on matter — five aggregates working so fast they look like one disc. When attention leaves the story of past and future and rests on raw sensation, the brain’s habit of manufacturing a narrator quiets. Some call that network the default mode. You do not need the name. You need the shift: from “who am I in this?” to “what is felt now?”

Dissolution can feel like freedom. It can also frighten the old character. If fear comes, it is another sensation. Do not turn it into a new religion called emptiness, and do not run back into a thicker story of me. Stay.

The path is traditionally described in stages. *Sotāpanna*, the stream-enterer, has seen enough that certain deep errors cannot again send one into the lower fields of existence. *Sakadāgāmī* returns once more, or finishes in this life. *Anāgāmī* does not return to this sensual world; a refined stage remains until the work is complete. The *arahant* has finished. These names are a map. Only knowledge is the land. Do not tattoo a rank on a self you are trying to see through.

Ignorance and saṅkhāra keep the wheel of mind and matter turning. Each new reaction writes another cause. Each moment of equanimity with *anicca* refuses to write it. That is enough philosophy. The rest is sitting, and living as if the sitting were true.

**Today.** Drop every fantasy of becoming an advanced meditator. For one sitting, be a beginner who feels what is here. Stream-entry is not an ambition. It is a result of not lying to yourself for long enough.
