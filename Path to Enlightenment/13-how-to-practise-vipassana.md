# Chapter 13 · How to practise Vipassanā

Why sensation? Because the blind mind lives in the body. Why Vipassanā? Because seeing things as they are, at the root of reaction, is how the root dies. Why the suttas? Because the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta is the map of that seeing.

After the mind can stay with the breath, you move attention through the body, part by part, then in sweeps, feeling whatever is there — heat, pulse, tightness, moisture, emptiness, pain, pleasure, nothing obvious. You do not invent sensation. You do not reject the blank areas. You wait, you feel, you move on.

- Work part by part; then sweep the mass of the body.
- If you like, one pass down with a breath, one pass up with a breath.
- If distracted, return to the breath, then continue.
- Do not camp on one part more than a few minutes.
- Move more quickly once the way is clear.
- When the surface is known, work more piercingly, through the parts.
- Do not pull the pleasant. Do not push the unpleasant.
- Stay in the present — *yathā bhūta*, as it actually is.
- Be kind to whatever comes. Kindness is not preference.

If dullness covers the scan, look at dullness itself with equanimity. If the mind wanders badly, rest it on the breath, then return. Use the breath at the start of a sitting; remain with equanimity during the sweep.

When the entire surface can be felt, penetrate through the body. Always with *anicca* felt, not chanted. In deep sitting the long words fall away. Keep the short ones: not permanent; no reaction; respect; law of nature. *Draṣṭā bhāv*, *sākṣī bhāv* — the stance of the witness.

If thoughts arise, put the mind back with the breath for a moment, then continue the sweep. Do nothing else. Observation is the action.

### When a pain zone will not move

A blind spot, a heavy block, a burning upper back, aching knees — these feel like concrete because the mind has labelled them “my pain.” Look closer. Is it actually one solid thing? Or heat, throb, pressure, tightness, and a brief coolness mixed together? Breaking the slab into raw ingredients is how *anicca* shows itself.

Do not sit there in order to make the pain leave. The moment the job becomes “so that it disappears,” you are practising aversion. Your only job is to watch it exist.

If a massive barrier holds you, stay with it neutrally for one or two minutes. If it does not shift or break into finer vibration, move on. Do not fight a battle of wills. Scan the rest of the body and return on the next pass.

Learn two kinds of pain. Meditation pain is dull ache, heat from posture, emotional shielding. Injury is sharp, stabbing, structural. If it is injury, change posture mindfully, without anger and without a story of failure.

**Today.** If you have been taught Vipassanā, give one sitting entirely to the rules above — especially not staying, not pulling, not pushing. If a stubborn zone appears, deconstruct it for two minutes, then move on. If you have not been taught, stay with the breath and the precepts until a qualified course trains you. This book will not initiate you. It will only tell you why the initiation matters.
